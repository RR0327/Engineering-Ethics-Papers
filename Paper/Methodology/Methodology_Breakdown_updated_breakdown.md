# Methodology Breakdown In Detail

### 1. Quantitative Cross-Sectional Survey

A bilingual Google Form containing 26 closed-ended questions was distributed through convenience sampling. A total of 154 valid responses were collected.

- **Respondent Profile:** Age group and daily web-application usage.
- **Privacy Awareness:** Concern about personal-data collection, tracking knowledge, and privacy-policy reading.
- **User Behaviour:** Cookie choices, permission decisions, website avoidance, and convenience-based acceptance.

### 2. Descriptive Data Analysis

The survey responses were analysed using frequencies, percentages, and charts to identify common behavioural and attitudinal patterns.

- **Consent Behaviour:** Acceptance, rejection, and avoidance patterns.
- **Interface Experience:** Hidden rejection choices, confusing settings, and perceived manipulation.
- **Trust and Regulation:** Trust in technology companies and support for stronger privacy protection.

### 3. Ethical Considerations and Limitations

Participation was voluntary, and personal identifiers should be removed before analysis. The findings represent self-reported responses and are mainly based on a young, convenience-based sample.

### 4. Data Preparation and Anonymization

Email addresses and unnecessary identifiers were removed. Responses were checked for duplicates, missing values, and inconsistent entries before analysis.

### 5. Statistical Relationship Analysis

Cross-tabulations, chi-square tests, and Cramér’s V may be used to examine relationships between privacy concern, awareness, consent behaviour, interface experience, and trust.

### 6. Reliability and Research Scope

Question wording, response-scale balance, and internal consistency were reviewed. Findings are limited by convenience sampling, age-group concentration, and reliance on self-reported behaviour.
