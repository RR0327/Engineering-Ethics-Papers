# `Results section`

1. **Demographics and Daily Usage Patterns**

- Age Distribution: The vast majority of the 154 respondents belong to the 18–24 age bracket (74.7%), followed by the 25–34 demographic (19.5%).
- Digital Consumption: Daily web and mobile application usage is notably high, with 50.6% of respondents reporting 2 to 5 hours of use, and an additional 31.1% reporting 6 or more hours daily.

2. **Privacy Awareness and Baseline Trust**

- General Privacy Concern: When asked about websites collecting personal information, 61.1% of respondents expressed being either moderately (31.2%) or slightly (29.9%) worried, while 18.2% were extremely worried.
- Knowledge of Third-Party Tracking: A combined 73.4% of users indicated that they understand the concept of "third-party data tracking" either well (32.5%) or somewhat (40.9%).
- Data Type Sensitivity: Respondents showed the highest concern for the sharing of private photos, videos, or device files (34.4%), followed by financial or transaction information (25.3%).
- Trust in Large Technology Companies: Trust levels are highly fragmented; 38.3% reported they "mostly trust" large technology companies to protect collected data, while 31.2% "mostly distrust" them. Furthermore, 86.4% of respondents stated that news of global data breaches has reduced their trust in web applications either significantly (49.4%) or somewhat (37.0%).

3. **Interactions with Consent Mechanisms and Dark Patterns**

- Cookie Banner Engagement: When presented with a cookie consent pop-up, 51.3% of users reported actively looking for a "Reject" button, whereas 26.0% stated they click "Accept All" immediately just to remove the barrier.
- Prevalence of Asymmetric UI: A significant majority reported encountering dark patterns frequently, with 66.2% stating they "always" (29.2%) or "often" (37.0%) see banners where "Accept All" is prominently colored while "Reject All" is hidden.
- Friction and Abandonment: Confusing or multi-layered privacy settings frequently discourage users, with 84.5% of respondents indicating they have abandoned changing settings or left a website entirely due to interface complexity (41.6% very frequently; 42.9% occasionally).
- Manipulation Awareness vs. Action: Even when aware that an interface is designed to manipulate them, 48.0% of users reported still clicking "Accept" just to proceed, either almost always (21.4%) or sometimes (26.6%).
- Interpretation of Warnings: When applications display warnings that denying permissions might "break core features," 47.4% of respondents interpret the text as an intentional tactic to induce a fear of missing out (FOMO), rather than a helpful technical explanation (19.5%).
- Policy Reading Habits: Engagement with Privacy Policies and Terms of Service is split: 29.2% claim to always read them, while a combined 42.2% admit to reading them rarely (26.6%) or never (15.6%).

4. **App Permissions and Mitigation Strategies**

- Installation Scrutiny: During new app installations, 51.3% of users check operational permission requests and only allow what is necessary, while 24.7% allow all requests simultaneously to save time.
- Handling Unjustified Requests: If a basic utility app (e.g., a flashlight) requests access to contacts, 92.8% of respondents take defensive action by either denying the permission while keeping the app (54.5%) or instantly deleting the application (38.3%).
- Efficacy of System Controls: Users exhibit skepticism regarding system-level controls. After disabling a permission in phone settings, only 18.8% are "very sure" that data tracking actually stops, while 36.4% remain either "not sure" or "not sure at all."
- Service Refusal: Over half of the respondents (52.6%) definitively stated they have refused to use a website or app specifically due to privacy concerns.

5. **Regulatory Perspectives and Ethical Expectations**

- Ethical Standards: A plurality of respondents (48.7%) strongly disagree or disagree that collecting user data without explicit consent is ethical, though 27.3% remain neutral.
- UI Equality Legislation: An overwhelming 79.9% of users agree (26.0%) or strongly agree (53.9%) that tracking rejection options should be just as easy to click as acceptance options.
- Corporate Accountability: There is strong support for legal penalties, with 87.6% of respondents agreeing or strongly agreeing that companies should face heavy fines for sharing data without explicit notification.
- Government Regulation: Similarly, 87.7% of users support stricter government laws and fines regarding unethical user data collection (61.7% strongly agree; 26.0% agree).
- Overall Satisfaction: Currently, 42.2% of respondents feel neutral about the privacy protections provided by modern web applications, while 24.7% are actively dissatisfied.
